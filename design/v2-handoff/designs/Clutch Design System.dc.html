Design System

One product, one moment: 2am, forty-eight hours out, stress at its peak. Every surface answers one question and offers one obvious next action. The system below is what makes that feel expensive instead of cheap — a near-black primary, an iris signal reserved for money, and a trust layer that never gets cleaned away.

05

The trust layer

The moat is not model access — it is the ability to say this is on the exam, here's why. Every ranked item, everywhere it appears, carries three things together: a confidence dot, a ★ if a past exam confirmed it, and a real citation. This is the one part of the system that may never be simplified for looks.

01 · CONFIDENCE DOT

Filled circle, tier-colored, optional halo of the tier's own tint. Sizes in px for app chrome; 0.62em inside the sheet so it scales with type.

02 · VERIFIED ★
★

Gold star, shown only when the item's pattern matched a real past exam in the pack. Stripped automatically when the past-exam PDF was image-only — an unearned ★ is worse than none.

Clear space, sizing, misuse

Clear space on all four sides equals the height of the folded corner — roughly a quarter of the mark's height. Nothing enters it, including the credits pill.
MIN SIZE Horizontal lockup no smaller than 104px wide. Below that the ruled lines close up — switch to the icon, which holds down to 16px.
APP CHROME Icon at 26px beside the Geist 600 wordmark at 15px — the lockup SVG is for marketing, the icon-plus-text pairing is for product chrome.
ON PHOTO Reversed only, and only over ink or a masked dark region. Never the colour lockup on an image.
NEVER Re-typeset the wordmark in Newsreader, add a shadow or outline, stretch it, rotate it, place the bolt in a colour the confidence tiers already own (green, gold, red carry meaning on every sheet), or set the mark on salmon.
FILES assets/brand/ — nine SVGs. The wordmark is live text set in Geist 700 with an Arial fallback; outline it before handing the files outside the team.
07

Laws

Tokens make things consistent; these make them feel expensive. Each one is a decision already made — treat them as settled.

01 One obvious action per screen. Exactly one ink button in view. Everything else is secondary, ghost, or a link. A second primary is a design bug.
02 Iris means money. Signal fills appear on upgrade, unlock, and Pro only. Spend it anywhere else and the paywall stops reading as a moment.
03 Border or shadow, never both loud. A hairline ink-150 border with sh-xs, or a shadow with no border. The bordered-and-floating "ghost card" is the fastest way to look cheap.
04 No left-stripe accents in app UI. Callouts get a tint and a hairline of their own hue. The colored left rule belongs to the printed sheet, where it encodes topic — different medium, different rules.
05 Evidence is always mono. Citations, confidence figures, file counts, page counts, credits. If it is a claim about provenance or measurement, it is Geist Mono — the reader learns to trust the shape.
06 The trust triplet is never trimmed. Dot, ★-if-verified, citation. Not on hover, not behind a "details" toggle, not dropped on mobile.
07 Serif for authority, sans for work. Newsreader on marketing headlines, sheet titles, modal headings, and the file-drop invitation. Never on a button, a label, or a table.
08 Nothing bounces. 120 / 200 / 360ms, one of two eases, no overshoot, no spring. A 1px lift is the entire hover vocabulary.
09 Marketing says cheat sheet. The product says reference sheet. "Cheat sheet" is search language and lives only on public pages. Inside the app and on the PDF it is an Exam Reference Sheet.
10 Voice: decisive, imperative, no hedging. "Read NOT and EXCEPT twice. They flip the answer." Not "you may want to consider re-reading."
11 Light mode only. The artifact lives on paper, so the product does too. The single exception is the generating screen — full ink-900, because it is a held breath, not a surface.
12 MAX means MAX. On the sheet, whitespace is waste. The page is always full, always top-ranked first, never padded to look designed.
THE TEST

Would a stressed student at 2am know what to do in five seconds, and believe the page?

If either answer is no, the screen is not finished — regardless of how well it follows everything above.
